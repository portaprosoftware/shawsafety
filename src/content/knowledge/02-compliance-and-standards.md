## What "UL 21S listed" actually means

UL 21S is a cable tie type classification under UL 62275, the standard for
Cable Management Systems. A Type 21S tie has to meet the same requirements as a
Type 21 tie — 100% retention of declared loop tensile strength after specified
environmental conditioning, plus material qualification under UL 746A
(short-term) and UL 746B (long-term) — with one added requirement: a minimum
declared loop tensile strength of 50 lb (220 N). Shaw Safety's 11" fluorescent
tie is declared at 75 lb (334 N), well above the 21S floor.

The 21S mark is what electrical inspectors, safety auditors, and terminal
operators look for when they need documentation that the tie is a certified
product and not a generic hardware-store tie.

## What UL 21S does NOT cover

UL 21S is a cable management certification. It is not a security seal
certification. Specifically:

- UL 21S does not certify the tie against ISO 17712 high-security seal
  requirements.
- UL 21S does not certify C-TPAT compliance.
- UL 21S does not certify tamper-evidence to WCO or CBP standards for
  international ocean container transit.
- UL 21S does not qualify the tie as a barrier seal.

For domestic drayage, LTL, and yard security where an indicative tamper-evident
seal is what the operator, terminal, or shipper is asking for, the UL 21S 75-lb
tie is the right product. For international ocean container shipping into or
out of a C-TPAT-certified supply chain, an ISO 17712 Class H bolt seal or cable
seal is required by CBP, not a plastic tie. Shaw Safety does not sell ISO 17712
Class H seals. Anyone who needs one should source it from a supplier of
high-security barrier seals; Shaw Safety's sales team can confirm the boundary
in writing but cannot supply that class of product.

## ISO 17712 — when Shaw Safety is not the right product

ISO 17712 is the international standard for mechanical freight container seals.
It defines three barrier classes: "I" indicative, "S" security, and "H" high
security. C-TPAT (Customs-Trade Partnership Against Terrorism) requires Class H
seals on all loaded containers bound for the United States by ocean. Class H
seals are hardened steel bolt seals or heavy cable seals tested by an ISO
17025-accredited third-party lab.

A plastic zip tie, regardless of tensile rating or color, is at most a Class I
indicative seal under ISO 17712 and cannot substitute for a Class H seal on an
international container. If a buyer is asking about C-TPAT, CBP-bound ocean
freight, or ISO 17712 compliance, the honest answer is that this tie does not
meet that requirement and they need a high-security barrier seal instead.

## What "DOT compliant" means for a security tie

There is no single "DOT seal certification" the way there is for hazmat
placards or FMVSS lighting. When Shaw Safety describes the tie as DOT
compliant, it means the product meets the material, marking, and
identifiability expectations that FMCSA inspectors and DOT roadside enforcement
apply to indicative security seals used on domestic trailer and intermodal
container doors: single-use construction, tamper-evident once seated, a legible
high-visibility color that can be verified from outside the cab, and a
traceable material declaration on file with the manufacturer.

If a specific customer, broker, or shipper has a written seal spec that names an
explicit DOT reference number, forward it and Shaw Safety will confirm the match
in writing before the order is placed.

## UL 62275 in one paragraph

UL 62275 is the North American harmonized cable management standard published
jointly by UL and CSA, aligned with IEC 62275. It defines six cable tie types
(1, 11, 2, 2S, 21, 21S) based on how strictly the tie is tested for tensile
retention, material properties, and thermal endurance. Types 21 and 21S sit at
the top for nonmetallic ties and are the two categories permitted for use in
air-handling plenums when marked AH-2. Shaw Safety's tie is qualified to 21S
and marked AH-2.

## What the AH-2 plenum rating means

AH-2 is a smoke and heat release classification under UL 62275. Ties marked
AH-2 have been tested to a specific minimum index for smoke generation and heat
contribution during a fire, and are approved by the NEC (Article 300.22) and
CEC for installation in return-air plenums and other air-handling spaces.

For freight use, the AH-2 rating is not directly relevant to sealing a trailer,
but it is a common purchasing filter for buyers who consolidate ordering across
facilities and want a single tie SKU that also passes electrical inspection
inside their warehouse.

## When indicative seals are the right tool

Indicative seals — the category Shaw Safety's tie sits in — are designed to
prove that a door was not opened between two known checkpoints, not to
physically resist a determined attacker. They are the correct product for:

- Domestic dry-van and refrigerated trailer sealing between a shipper and a
  receiver.
- Intermodal drayage between a rail terminal and a nearby distribution center.
- Yard checks where a driver moves a loaded trailer across a facility.
- LTL and multi-stop routes where the seal is verified at each stop.
- Line-haul freight moving within the U.S. and not crossing an international
  boundary in a sealed container.

## Tamper evidence

The Shaw Safety tie is a one-time-use, self-locking pawl design. Once the tail
is drawn through the head, the pawl engages and cannot be released without
cutting the tie. The head cannot be reopened by hand, prying, or heating
without leaving obvious damage to the head casing or the pawl teeth.

Any attempt to defeat the seal will show one of three signatures: a visibly
deformed head, a partially cut and re-tied body, or a color mismatch if the tie
was replaced with a substitute. This is why fluorescent color assignment per
lane or shift matters — a swap is instantly visible even without checking a
serial number.

## Written seal specification match

If a terminal, broker, or receiver has a written seal specification — for
example, an SOP that requires "an 11-inch indicative security seal, UL 21S
listed, minimum 50-lb tensile, fluorescent color" — send the specification to
Shaw Safety before ordering. A team member will compare the spec to the current
product datasheet and reply in writing confirming the match, or flag any line
item that does not conform, before the purchase is committed. This step is free
and typically takes less than one business day.
