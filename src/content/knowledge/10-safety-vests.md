## Product overview

Shaw Safety sells ANSI/ISEA 107 Class 2 Type R hi-vis vests alongside the
fluorescent tie catalog. The vest is designed for the same operator profile: a
trucking company, a drayage carrier, or a roll-off, septic, or portable
sanitation company that needs to keep drivers compliant with roadway
high-visibility requirements without paying wholesale-plus-markup at an
industrial safety supplier. Vests are sold in crew-sized 10-packs.

## Standards and compliance

- ANSI/ISEA 107-2020 Class 2 background material
- Type R (Roadway) — the class of garment required for workers exposed to public
  roadway traffic
- OSHA 1926.201 compatible — the OSHA construction standard section that
  references high-visibility apparel for workers in traffic control zones
- 2-inch retroreflective banding at ANSI-conformant placement
- 5-point breakaway construction

## What ANSI/ISEA 107 Class 2 means

ANSI/ISEA 107 is the American National Standard for High-Visibility Safety
Apparel. Class 2 is the intermediate performance class — more fluorescent
background material and more retroreflective tape than Class 1, less than
Class 3. Class 2 is the legal minimum for workers exposed to traffic on public
roadways at typical highway speeds.

OSHA's construction regulations and the Federal Highway Administration's worker
visibility rule reference ANSI/ISEA 107 Class 2 as the baseline for anyone
working in the right-of-way of a Federal-aid highway. In practice, Class 2 is
what a DOT inspector or an OSHA compliance officer expects to see on a driver
hooking up a trailer at a roadside.

## What Type R means

The 2015 revision of ANSI/ISEA 107 introduced garment type designations based on
the wearer's operating environment. Type R (Roadway) is for workers exposed to
vehicular traffic on public access roads or in temporary traffic control zones.
Type P (Public Safety) is for emergency responders and law enforcement whose
apparel needs are different. Type O (Off-Road) is for workers not exposed to
public traffic. A trucking, drayage, or waste operation always wants Type R.

## OSHA 1926.201 compatibility

OSHA 29 CFR 1926.201 covers signaling and flagging in construction zones. The
regulation references the MUTCD (Manual on Uniform Traffic Control Devices),
which in turn references ANSI/ISEA 107 Class 2 or Class 3 as the visibility
standard for flaggers and workers exposed to traffic. The Shaw Safety vest meets
Class 2 Type R and is compatible with these requirements.

For flaggers on high-speed roadways over 50 mph, Class 3 is often required —
Class 3 has full-sleeve coverage and is a separate product not currently in the
Shaw Safety catalog.

## 5-point breakaway construction

The vest is built with 5-point breakaway seams — the shoulder seams, side seams,
and center front will release under a specified pull load. Breakaway
construction is a safety feature for workers who could get caught on moving
equipment (a trailer door, a forklift mast, a lifting boom). The vest releases
before the wearer is dragged. Breakaway is standard on modern Type R roadway
vests and is expected by most fleet safety programs.

## Sizing

Standard adult sizing runs from Small through 5XL. Vests are sized generously to
allow for a jacket or coverall underneath in cold weather; a driver who wants a
slim fit should typically size down from their normal shirt size. Detailed
measurements are on the size chart on the product page. For fleets standardizing
across a mixed crew, single-size 10-packs are available on request through
sales.

## Colors

The vests come in Hi-Vis Lime (fluorescent yellow-green) and Hi-Vis Orange
(fluorescent orange-red). Both are ANSI-conformant. Lime is the standard choice
and the highest-contrast option against most roadside backgrounds. Orange is
preferred where the local background is dominated by green vegetation —
roadside brush, agricultural work zones — or where a fleet's other safety gear
is already color-coded to orange.

## Care and washing

- Machine washable in cold water on a gentle cycle.
- Do not bleach — bleach degrades both the fluorescent pigment and the
  retroreflective tape.
- Tumble dry low or line dry.
- Do not iron the retroreflective tape.

Retire vests when the retroreflective tape shows cracking, delamination, or a
visible loss of reflectivity under a flashlight, or when the fluorescent
background material has faded. A vest that has lost its reflectivity no longer
meets the ANSI standard even if it looks superficially fine in daylight.

## Expected useful life

For a driver who wears the vest for a full shift five days a week and washes it
weekly, expect approximately 6 months of ANSI-conformant life before the
retroreflective performance starts to degrade below spec. Rougher use — heavy
dirt, frequent snags, weekly bleach cycles — will cut that life significantly.

For a fleet running a strict compliance program, plan on replacing vests every
6 months regardless of visible condition. At $6.19 per vest at the deepest tier,
this is a small line item.

## Vest-only compliance is not enough

A Class 2 vest alone does not satisfy every roadside safety requirement.
High-speed roadways over 50 mph typically require Class 3, which means
full-sleeve coverage. Night work in certain jurisdictions requires additional
retroreflective placement. Local terminal or shipper policies may layer
additional requirements on top of the OSHA/ANSI baseline.

Check the specific requirement for the work being done and choose the correct
class — a Class 2 vest is the right answer for most drayage, LTL, waste, and
service-truck operations, but not for every application.

## When to choose a vest vs a jacket

For hot-weather work or short exposure at a dock or roadside, the vest is the
standard. For prolonged outdoor exposure in cold weather, a hi-vis jacket or a
parka with ANSI-compliant coloring and reflectivity is a better choice. The Shaw
Safety vest is designed to be worn over a driver's normal work clothing,
including a light jacket or a work shirt.
