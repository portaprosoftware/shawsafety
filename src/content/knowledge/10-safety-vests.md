## What ANSI/ISEA 107 Class 2 means

ANSI/ISEA 107 is the American National Standard for High-Visibility Safety Apparel. Class 2 is the intermediate performance class — more fluorescent background material and more retroreflective tape than Class 1, less than Class 3. Class 2 is the legal minimum for workers exposed to traffic on public roadways at typical highway speeds. OSHA's construction regulations and the Federal Highway Administration's worker visibility rule reference ANSI/ISEA 107 Class 2 as the baseline for anyone working in the right-of-way of a Federal-aid highway. In practice, Class 2 is what a DOT inspector or an OSHA compliance officer expects to see on a driver hooking up a trailer at a roadside.

## What Type R means

The 2015 revision of ANSI/ISEA 107 introduced garment type designations based on the wearer's operating environment. Type R (Roadway) is for workers exposed to vehicular traffic on public access roads or in temporary traffic control zones. Type P (Public Safety) is for emergency responders and law enforcement whose apparel needs are different. Type O (Off-Road) is for workers not exposed to public traffic. A trucking, drayage, or waste operation always wants Type R.

## OSHA 1926.201 compatibility

OSHA 29 CFR 1926.201 covers signaling and flagging in construction zones. The regulation references the MUTCD (Manual on Uniform Traffic Control Devices) which in turn references ANSI/ISEA 107 Class 2 or Class 3 as the visibility standard for flaggers and workers exposed to traffic. The Shaw Safety vest meets Class 2 Type R and is compatible with these requirements. For flaggers on high-speed roadways over 50 mph, Class 3 is often required — Class 3 has full-sleeve coverage and is a separate product not currently in the Shaw Safety catalog.

## 5-point breakaway construction

The vest is built with 5-point breakaway seams — the shoulder seams, side seams, and center front will release under a specified pull load. Breakaway construction is a safety feature for workers who could get caught on moving equipment (a trailer door, a forklift mast, a lifting boom). The vest releases before the wearer is dragged. Breakaway is standard on modern Type R roadway vests and is expected by most fleet safety programs.

## Care and washing

- Machine washable in cold water on a gentle cycle.
- Do not bleach — bleach degrades both the fluorescent pigment and the retroreflective tape.
- Tumble dry low or line dry.
- Do not iron the retroreflective tape.
- Retire vests when the retroreflective tape shows cracking, delamination, or a visible loss of reflectivity under a flashlight, or when the fluorescent background material has faded. A vest that has lost its reflectivity no longer meets the ANSI standard even if it looks superficially fine in daylight.

## Expected useful life

For a driver who wears the vest for a full shift five days a week and washes it weekly, expect approximately 6 months of ANSI-conformant life before the retroreflective performance starts to degrade below spec. Rougher use — heavy dirt, frequent snags, weekly bleach cycles — will cut that life significantly. For a fleet running a strict compliance program, plan on replacing vests every 6 months regardless of visible condition. At $6.19 per vest at volume, this is a small line item.

## Vest-only compliance is not enough

A Class 2 vest alone does not satisfy every roadside safety requirement. High-speed roadways over 50 mph typically require Class 3, which means full-sleeve coverage. Night work in certain jurisdictions requires additional retroreflective placement. Local terminal or shipper policies may layer additional requirements on top of the OSHA/ANSI baseline. Check the specific requirement for the work being done and choose the correct class — a Class 2 vest is the right answer for most drayage, LTL, waste, and service-truck operations but not for every application.

## When to choose a vest vs a jacket

For hot-weather work or short exposure at a dock or roadside, the vest is the standard. For prolonged outdoor exposure in cold weather, a hi-vis jacket or a parka with ANSI-compliant coloring and reflectivity is a better choice. The Shaw Safety vest is designed to be worn over a driver's normal work clothing, including a light jacket or a work shirt.
