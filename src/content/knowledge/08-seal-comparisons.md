## The security seal landscape

Cargo security seals fall into three broad classes under ISO 17712: indicative
(I), security (S), and high security (H). Indicative seals — the category Shaw
Safety's fluorescent tie sits in — prove a door was not opened between
checkpoints and can be defeated by an attacker with tools. Security seals resist
casual attack. High-security seals are hardened bolt seals or heavy cable seals
that require bolt cutters and are required by C-TPAT for loaded ocean containers
bound for the United States.

Different applications call for different classes. The most common purchasing
mistake is buying up (over-specifying) for a domestic route where an indicative
tie is what the terminal actually wants, or buying down (under-specifying) for
an international ocean shipment where CBP requires a Class H bolt seal.

## Shaw Safety fluorescent tie vs generic hardware-store zip tie

The visible difference is color; the real difference is certification and
consistency.

- **Shaw Safety:** UL 62275 Type 21S listed, 75-lb declared tensile with the
  full material and thermal qualification behind it, fluorescent pigment sized
  for legibility at distance, lot traceability, spec sheet with tolerance
  callouts.
- **Generic zip tie:** no UL type declaration or a lower one, tensile stated
  without a certified test method, non-fluorescent colors that fade quickly,
  no lot traceability.

A safety manager or inspector accepts a Type 21S product without a second look.
A generic tie routinely triggers a follow-up question.

## Shaw Safety fluorescent tie vs bolt seal

A bolt seal is a steel pin capped inside a metal housing, sized to fit through a
container door handle keeper. Bolt seals are the correct product for
international ocean freight, high-value domestic loads, and any lane that
requires an ISO 17712 Class H seal.

- **Shaw Safety tie:** 75-lb tensile, cut with a small hand tool, roughly
  2–3 cents per tie, indicative class.
- **Bolt seal:** several-thousand-pound tensile, requires bolt cutters to
  remove, typically $1–$3 per seal depending on volume, high-security class.

Shaw Safety does not sell bolt seals. If a lane requires ISO 17712 Class H, that
seal has to be sourced from a high-security barrier seal supplier. For sealing a
domestic dry van between two U.S. facilities, the fluorescent tie is the more
sensible product.

## Shaw Safety fluorescent tie vs cable seal

A cable seal is a steel cable with a locking body. Cable seals sit between
indicative and high security depending on cable diameter — 1.5 mm to 3.5 mm is
light-duty indicative use, 3.5 mm to 5 mm is medium-duty, and 5 mm and above
with proper certification is ISO 17712 Class H.

- **Shaw Safety tie:** fastest to install, easiest to inspect, cheapest per
  unit, indicative only.
- **Cable seal:** more flexible than a bolt seal for irregular hardware, longer
  tail for wrap-around applications, higher tensile options available.

For a fleet running domestic drayage and yard moves that wants one product on
the shelf, the fluorescent tie is simpler. Shaw Safety does not carry cable
seals.

## Shaw Safety fluorescent tie vs pull-tight seal

A pull-tight seal is a plastic strip with a locking head, similar in form factor
to a zip tie but typically sold with a printed serial number and a lower tensile
rating targeted at trailer door use rather than cable management. Pull-tight
seals compete directly with the Shaw Safety fluorescent tie in the
indicative-seal category.

- **Shaw Safety:** 75-lb tensile (higher than most pull-tight seals), Type 21S
  UL listing (broader than a trailer-only pull-tight seal), fluorescent color at
  high-vis pigment concentration, and an 11-inch length that fits both trailer
  hardware and intermodal container hardware.
- **Standard pull-tight seal:** often 40- to 60-lb tensile, no UL type listing,
  printed serial number as a standard feature.

The Shaw Safety tie is a more versatile SKU across a mixed fleet. If
pre-printed sequential numbering is a strict requirement, ask sales what is
currently available.

## Shaw Safety fluorescent tie vs meter or utility seal

Meter and utility seals are small indicative devices used to seal electric, gas,
and water meters against tamper. They are a different form factor and are not
interchangeable with the Shaw Safety trailer and container tie. Shaw Safety does
not sell meter seals.

## Shaw Safety fluorescent tie vs padlock

A padlock is a physical barrier — the tie is a tamper indicator. Many domestic
fleets use both: a padlock as the primary lock and a colored fluorescent tie as
the tamper indicator on the second door handle or the vertical bar. The tie does
not replace the padlock and the padlock does not replace the tie; they solve
different problems. This dual-use pattern is common on high-value domestic loads
that do not rise to the level of C-TPAT ocean freight.

## Product selection quick reference

Where the Shaw Safety fluorescent tie is the right product:

- Domestic dry van, refrigerated trailer, LTL, drayage.
- Yard moves, spotter operations, lot transfers.
- Waste, recycling, roll-off.
- Portable sanitation and septic service accountability.
- Air cargo ULD sealing on domestic legs.

Where it is not, and a different class of seal is required:

- International ocean container or a C-TPAT lane — requires an ISO 17712
  Class H bolt or cable seal.
- High-value domestic freight where the shipper's policy specifies a
  security-class barrier seal.
- Meter and utility sealing — a different form factor entirely.

Shaw Safety's catalog covers the first list. For anything on the second list,
sales will say so directly rather than stretching the product to fit.
