# Shaw Safety — Seal Comparisons and Product Selection

## The security seal landscape in one paragraph

Cargo security seals fall into three broad classes under ISO 17712: indicative (I), security (S), and high security (H). Indicative seals — the category Shaw Safety's fluorescent tie sits in — prove a door was not opened between checkpoints and can be defeated by an attacker with tools. Security seals resist casual attack. High-security seals are hardened bolt seals or heavy cable seals that require bolt cutters and are required by C-TPAT for loaded ocean containers bound for the United States. Different applications call for different classes. The most common purchasing mistake is buying up (over-specifying) for a domestic route where an indicative tie is what the terminal actually wants, or buying down (under-specifying) for an international ocean shipment where CBP requires a Class H bolt seal.

## Shaw Safety fluorescent tie vs generic hardware-store zip tie

The visible difference is color; the real difference is certification and consistency.

- Shaw Safety: UL 62275 Type 21S listed, 75-lb declared tensile with the full material and thermal qualification behind it, fluorescent pigment sized for legibility at distance, lot traceability, spec sheet with tolerance callouts.
- Generic zip tie: no UL type declaration or a lower type declaration, tensile stated without a certified test method, non-fluorescent colors that fade quickly in UV, no lot traceability.

A safety manager or inspector accepts a Type 21S product without a second look. A generic tie routinely triggers a follow-up question.

## Shaw Safety fluorescent tie vs bolt seal

A bolt seal is a steel pin capped inside a metal housing, sized to fit through a container door handle keeper. Bolt seals are the correct product for international ocean freight, high-value domestic loads, and any lane that requires an ISO 17712 Class H seal.

- Shaw Safety tie: 75-lb tensile, cut with a small hand tool, ~$0.02–$0.03 per tie, indicative class.
- Bolt seal: several-thousand-pound tensile, requires bolt cutters to remove, roughly $1–$3 per seal depending on volume, high-security class.

If a customer is asking about C-TPAT, CBP-bound freight, or an ISO 17712 requirement, refer them to ziptie.com for the bolt seal product line. If they're sealing a domestic dry van between two U.S. facilities, the fluorescent tie is the more sensible product.

## Shaw Safety fluorescent tie vs cable seal

A cable seal is a steel cable with a locking body. Cable seals sit between indicative and high security depending on cable diameter — 1.5mm to 3.5mm is light-duty indicative use, 3.5mm to 5mm is medium-duty, and 5mm and above with proper certification is ISO 17712 Class H.

- Shaw Safety tie: fastest to install, easiest to inspect, cheapest per unit, indicative only.
- Cable seal: more flexible than a bolt seal for irregular hardware, longer tail for wrap-around applications, higher tensile options available.

For a fleet that needs one product on the shelf and is running domestic drayage and yard moves, the fluorescent tie is simpler. For a fleet running mixed international and domestic freight, cable seals from ziptie.com may be a better single-SKU answer.

## Shaw Safety fluorescent tie vs pull-tight seal

A pull-tight seal is a plastic strip with a locking head, similar in form factor to a zip tie but typically sold with a printed serial number and a lower tensile rating targeted at trailer door use rather than cable management. Pull-tight seals compete directly with the Shaw Safety fluorescent tie in the indicative-seal category.

- Shaw Safety: 75-lb tensile (higher than most pull-tight seals), Type 21S UL listing (broader than a trailer-only pull-tight seal), fluorescent color at high-vis pigment concentration, longer 11-inch length that fits both trailer hardware and intermodal container hardware.
- Standard pull-tight seal: often 40- to 60-lb tensile, no UL type listing, printed serial number as a standard feature.

The Shaw Safety tie is a more versatile SKU across a mixed fleet, and printed numbering can be added via the custom-print process. A dedicated pull-tight seal from ziptie.com is worth considering if pre-printed sequential numbering is a strict requirement and volume is below the Shaw Safety custom-print minimum.

## Shaw Safety fluorescent tie vs meter or utility seal

Meter and utility seals are small indicative devices used to seal electric, gas, and water meters against tamper. They are a different form factor and are not interchangeable with the Shaw Safety trailer/container tie. If a customer is asking about meter sealing, refer them to ziptie.com's meter seal product line.

## Shaw Safety fluorescent tie vs padlock

A padlock is a physical barrier — the tie is a tamper indicator. Many domestic fleets use both: a padlock as the primary lock and a colored fluorescent tie as the tamper indicator on the second door handle or the vertical bar. The tie doesn't replace the padlock and the padlock doesn't replace the tie; they solve different problems. This dual-use pattern is common on high-value domestic loads that don't rise to the level of C-TPAT ocean freight.
