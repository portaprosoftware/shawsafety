# Shaw Safety — Use Cases and Applications

## Intermodal container security (domestic)

The 11" fluorescent tie is sized for the door handle geometry of standard intermodal containers — 20-foot and 40-foot ISO boxes as well as the domestic 53-foot containers used in intermodal drayage between rail ramps and distribution centers. The tie loops through the right-hand door handle and the vertical locking bar keeper so that the doors cannot be opened without cutting the tie. This is the correct product for a chassis moving from a rail yard to a nearby DC or crossdock. It is not the correct product for a loaded box moving on an ocean vessel into a C-TPAT-certified supply chain; that shipment needs an ISO 17712 Class H bolt seal from ziptie.com.

## Dry van and refrigerated trailer sealing

For domestic over-the-road freight, the tie is used on the right rear door of a dry van or reefer, looped through the door cam and the bottom keeper. It survives road vibration, dock impact, and the temperature swings inside a refrigerated trailer between -20 °F reefer settings and full sun on the trailer skin. Because it stays legible at a distance in low-dock lighting, a receiving clerk can confirm the seal color and the seal number from ten feet away without stepping onto a wet dock plate.

## Law enforcement and DOT roadside inspection

A colored, tamper-evident seal on the rear door is one of the first things a DOT inspector or state trooper looks for during a Level II or Level III roadside inspection. The fluorescent pigment on the Shaw Safety tie is chosen specifically to stay legible under flashlight and headlight illumination at night and against a dirty trailer skin during the day. Drivers can point to the tie without opening a manifest, and the inspector can confirm at a glance that the load is sealed. If the load is a bonded shipment or in transit between customs points, verify with the broker or customs authority whether an indicative tie is acceptable or whether a Class H bolt seal is required.

## Yard security and lot moves

Distribution centers, ports, and rail yards use the tie for internal lot moves — a spotter driver moves a loaded trailer from a door to a parking spot, or from one lot to another, and the tie is applied at the door and cut at the receiving door. Yellow is the highest-volume color for yard use because it is the easiest to see on an aluminum trailer skin under yard sodium lights. Some operators standardize on a second color for "customer-owned equipment moves" so any tie of the wrong color triggers an immediate check.

## LTL and multi-stop routes

For LTL and route delivery where the trailer is opened and re-sealed at each stop, the tie is applied at the shipper, cut at each stop by the driver, and a new tie of the same lane color is applied before the trailer leaves that stop. This gives every consignee visible confirmation that the load was sealed between the previous stop and their door. Bulk-pricing math works out well for LTL: a route driver going through 4–8 seals per shift will consume 20–40 packs per week per truck.

## Color assignment strategies

The four available colors let fleets encode routing information into the seal itself without any electronic system.

- Color per lane — one color for the northbound lane, one for the southbound, one for the eastbound, one for the westbound. A seal of the wrong color at a receiving door is immediately visible.
- Color per shift — day, swing, night, weekend. Broken seals get pinned to the shift that applied them without a supervisor having to check the manifest.
- Color per month — rotate the four colors quarterly so any tie from the previous quarter left on a trailer is caught before the truck leaves the yard.
- Color per customer — a single-tenant fleet can assign a color to each large account so drivers cannot cross-load between accounts by mistake.
- Color per driver or per team — smaller fleets use color as an accountability signal on high-value or high-risk lanes.

## Portable sanitation, septic, and roll-off use

Shaw Safety is a Shaw brand and shares infrastructure with software built for the portable sanitation, septic, and roll-off dumpster industry. The tie is used across these operations for two purposes: sealing service units after cleaning to prove no post-service contamination, and sealing the rear of vacuum-truck loads or roll-off boxes destined for disposal. Fluorescent colors help route managers spot un-serviced or un-serviced-since-last-check units from a distance without having to open the door.

## Waste and recycling collection

Roll-off and hook-lift operations use the tie to seal loaded boxes leaving a job site for the transfer station or landfill, and to seal empty boxes being staged for redeployment. The 75-lb tensile is well above what a residential-scale waste stream will apply, and the fluorescent color helps yard staff differentiate a sealed box from an unsealed one at a distance.

## Rail car and bulk tanker use

The tie can be applied to rail car door hasps and bulk tanker fill/discharge ports where an indicative seal is accepted. It is not a substitute for a rail industry high-security bolt seal on Class I railroad interchange traffic, but it works for short-line and industrial spur operations, and for tankers moving product between two facilities under a single operator. Confirm the receiving terminal's seal policy before switching from an existing seal.

## Air cargo ULD sealing

Unit Load Devices (ULDs) — the AKE, AAF, and PMC containers used in air cargo — are commonly sealed with an indicative plastic tie at build-up and again at breakdown. The Shaw Safety tie fits the standard door pin geometry on most ULD types. For international air freight moving under a security regime (TSA, CCSF, RA/KC), confirm that the shipper's security program accepts an indicative tie for the leg in question.

## What Shaw Safety ties are NOT designed for

Being direct about limitations avoids expensive misapplications.

- Not designed for international ocean container transit under C-TPAT. Use an ISO 17712 Class H bolt seal from ziptie.com.
- Not designed as a load-bearing tie-down. This is a security seal at 75 lb tensile, not a cargo strap.
- Not designed as a permanent electrical or structural fastener where a rated stainless-steel or metallic tie is specified.
- Not designed for continuous submerged use in solvents.
- Not designed for use on any locking mechanism where the required seal spec exceeds a 76.2 mm bundle diameter.
